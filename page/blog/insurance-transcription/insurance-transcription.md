# What Is Insurance Transcription and How Does It Work?

**Lede:** Turn recorded statements and claims calls into searchable text, with an AI draft from TranscriptX and a checklist for the details that decide a claim.

> **Quick answer:** Insurance transcription turns recorded statements, claims calls and adjuster interviews into written text for the claim file. The fastest way is to upload the recording to [TranscriptX Audio to Text](/audio-to-text/), get a timestamped draft in seconds, then check every date, amount and name against the audio before you rely on it.

Insurance transcription is about one thing: a written record that says exactly what the person said. A claim can turn on a single detail, such as whether the car was parked or moving, or whether the leak started on the 3rd or the 13th. If the transcript gets that wrong, everything built on it is wrong too.

This guide covers what insurance transcription is, what gets transcribed in a claim, how to transcribe a recorded statement step by step with AI, what to check, how to format the transcript, and when an insurance transcription service or a court reporter is still the right call.

## What Is Insurance Transcription?

Insurance transcription is the process of converting spoken insurance content, mostly recorded calls and interviews, into written text. The transcript goes into the claim or policy file, where adjusters, managers, investigators and lawyers can read, search and quote it.

It is used across the whole industry:

- **Claims teams:** adjusters and claims handlers who take recorded statements.
- **Special investigation units (SIU):** investigators reviewing interviews on suspicious claims.
- **Agents and brokers:** client calls about coverage, renewals and changes to a policy.
- **Underwriting:** phone interviews with applicants, for example for life or health cover.
- **Legal and litigation:** defense counsel, paralegals and in-house lawyers working on disputed claims.
- **Training and quality:** call reviews and team meetings.

## What Gets Transcribed in an Insurance Claim?

Most insurance claims transcription comes from a handful of recording types. Each one has its own risk, so it helps to know which one you are working with.

| Recording | Who is speaking | What the transcript is used for |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded statement | Adjuster and the insured, claimant or witness | The person's account of what happened, in their own words |
| First notice of loss call | Customer and call center agent | When and how the loss was reported |
| Customer voicemail | Customer | Reports, updates and complaints left by phone |
| Witness interview | Investigator and witness | Checking one account against another |
| Expert or medical call | Adjuster and doctor, engineer or appraiser | Facts about damage, injury or cost |
| Examination under oath (EUO) | Attorney, insured, court reporter | A formal sworn record, usually transcribed by the court reporter |
| Claim review meeting | Claims team, managers | Decisions and next steps |

For short voice messages, [Voicemail to Text](/voicemail-to-text/) is the quickest route. For longer statements and interviews, use [Audio to Text](/audio-to-text/) or [Interview Transcription](/interview-transcription/).

With AI, transcribing a recorded statement takes a few minutes of your time, and most of that is checking rather than typing. Typing up one hour of audio by hand takes about 4 hours. Here is the workflow with TranscriptX.

## What Should You Check in an Insurance Transcript?

Check every detail a decision depends on, not the whole transcript word by word. Speech recognition handles everyday speech well; the risk sits in details that sound alike or that change the meaning of the claim.

| What to check | Why it goes wrong | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Dates and times | "Third" and "thirteenth", "five" and "nine" sound alike on a phone line | Loss on March 3 vs March 13 |
| Amounts | "Fifteen" and "fifty", "hundred" and "thousand" | $1,500 vs $5,000 repair estimate |
| Policy and claim numbers | Long strings of digits and letters said quickly | A transposed digit in the policy number |
| Names and addresses | Unusual names, street names and spellings | The witness's surname, the location of the loss |
| Vehicles and property | Makes, models, plates, rooms and items | Which car was hit, which room flooded |
| Injuries and treatment | Medical terms, body parts and left versus right | Left knee vs right knee |
| Negatives | "Didn't", "wasn't" and "never" can drop out when said quietly | "I wasn't on the phone" |
| Who said what | Crosstalk on a three-way call | The adjuster's question recorded as the claimant's answer |

Two habits save time:

1. **Compare with the claim file.** Check dates, amounts and policy numbers against the documents you already have. When the spoken detail and the file differ, keep what was said and add a note. That difference may matter.
2. **Mark what you can't hear instead of guessing.** Write `[inaudible 00:12:41]` with the time in the recording. A guessed word in a statement is worse than a gap.

## How Should an Insurance Transcript Be Formatted?

Start with a header that identifies the claim, give each speaker their own paragraph and keep the timestamps. That makes the transcript easy to file, search and quote later.

A simple header for a recorded statement:

```
Claim number: CLM-000000
Policy number: POL-000000
Type: Recorded statement of [name], [insured / claimant / witness]
Date and time of recording: 5 November 2026, 10:30
Taken by: [adjuster name], by phone
Consent to record: confirmed on the recording at 00:00:20
Transcribed: TranscriptX draft, checked against the audio by [your name]
```

Then the body, one paragraph per speaker turn:

```
[00:03:12] Adjuster: Where was your vehicle when the other car hit it?
[00:03:16] Claimant: It was stopped at the light on Main Street. I wasn't moving.
```

A few more conventions:

- **Keep the opening and closing of the statement.** The part where the person agrees to be recorded, and any questions asked at the end, belong in the transcript.
- **Use one file per recording,** named the same way each time, for example `CLM-000000_recorded-statement_claimant_2026-11-05.docx`.
- **Use clean verbatim for most work.** Remove "um" and false starts, but keep every fact exactly as said. Use full verbatim, with pauses and hesitations, when the case may be disputed and the way something was said could matter.

For day-to-day claims work, AI transcription plus your own check is enough and much faster. Insurance transcription services and companies with human transcribers make sense when you need someone else to do the checking or need a verified transcript.

## When Do You Need Legal Transcription for an Insurance Case?

When the transcript itself may become evidence. Legal transcription for insurance cases is usually needed for examinations under oath, depositions, hearings and disputed claims heading to court. These normally need a court reporter or a legal transcription service that can certify the transcript.

An AI draft is still useful around those cases. Lawyers and adjusters use it to:

- Prepare for an EUO or deposition by reviewing earlier recorded statements.
- Find the exact moment something was said before ordering a formal transcript.
- Compare one witness's account with another.

If you are not sure which kind of transcript a case needs, ask the attorney handling it before you order anything.

## Is It Safe to Transcribe Insurance Calls with AI?

Check your company's policy first. Insurers often have rules about which tools may handle claimant data, medical details or other personal information, and some require approved vendors only.

Some practical rules:

- **Get consent to record,** in line with the rules where you and the other person are. Most recorded statements confirm consent at the start of the recording.
- **Keep sensitive data inside approved tools.** If your compliance team hasn't approved a tool, don't upload medical records or other protected information to it.
- **Know what happens to your files.** Read the provider's privacy policy. For TranscriptX, see the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/).
- **Remove what the reader doesn't need.** Before you share a transcript outside the claims team, take out bank details, ID numbers and other personal data that isn't relevant.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an insurance recorded statement?

It is a recorded interview, usually by phone, in which an adjuster asks the insured, the claimant or a witness to describe what happened. The recording and its transcript become part of the claim file.

### Is AI transcription accurate enough for insurance claims?

On clear recordings, it gives you a solid draft. Dates, amounts, names and policy numbers still need a check against the audio, because one wrong detail can change a claim.

### Can I transcribe a recorded statement for free?

Yes. TranscriptX is free every day and you can try it without an account. Speaker labels and noise reduction are on the [Pro plan](/pricing/).

### Can TranscriptX tell the adjuster and the claimant apart?

On the Pro plan, TranscriptX labels the different speakers automatically. Rename the labels to "Adjuster" and "Claimant" while you check the transcript.

### Which file format should I use for the claim file?

Use .docx if you will edit the transcript or add it to a report, .pdf for a fixed copy to file or share, and .txt for plain text or other software.

### Can you work from home transcribing for insurance companies?

Yes. Insurance transcription services and some insurers hire transcriptionists, often remotely. Most look for fast, accurate typing, care with names and numbers, and experience with legal or medical terms.
